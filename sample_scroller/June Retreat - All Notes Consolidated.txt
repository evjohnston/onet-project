Debate 1
"Whoever is first in AI will dominate everything else"


Affirm: Table 1 · Refute: Table 6 
Speakers: Phil + Greg Sands (affirm), Chris Manning (refute) 
Also recorded by Tables 4, 5, 7
Affirm side
As recorded by Table 1 (affirm's own worksheet):
   * First = compounding effect of forming an ecosystem
   * Everything = economic + science (+ military)
   * Although there are races within the same ecosystem (e.g., DeepMind, Anthropic), we can contain them in a single ecosystem
As recorded by Table 6 (the refuting table's notes on affirm's claims):
   * We have to question what we mean by "first"; is this AGI or something more specific?
   * The definition that makes the most sense is being the first to achieve a durable and compounding advantage
   * If you have a particular level of sophistication in AI, it will enable further development
   * What does dominating mean?
   * Economics, science, and the "things that matter" are where artificial intelligence will step into the places that need intelligence
   * First movers will have what they are doing seen as a general technological enabler
   * The first mover will establish the norms, geopolitical norms, and so being the first mover will enable faster technology and diffusion into other domains as well
As recorded by Table 4 ("First movers have advantage" — PRO):
   * What does being first mean?
   * First to achieve a durable and compound advantage
   * Reason to suspect that AI becomes an enabler of AI
   * Dominating every single thing or just economics and science, the things that matter
   * Reason for dominance: first movers are going to see what they are doing as a general technological enabler — electricity, computers, etc. AI can advance thinking in every domain
   * Flywheel that happens when AI becomes the enabler, so the first mover will accelerate their advantage and set geopolitical standards and norms everyone else will have to follow
   * Not just faster technology, but faster diffusion into every domain
As recorded by Table 5 (supplementary, Debate 1):
   * Phil: Being the first to achieve a durable and compounding advantage; with good-enough AI, AI becomes an enabler for AI; general technological enablement by AI; self-accelerating AI
   * Greg: Assert compounding advantage; soft-power effect of AI on all sectors
As recorded by Table 3 (notes on debates):
   * Pro: durable and COMPOUNDING advantage — in spaces like economics, science, etc.; electricity, computers = general technological enabler; flywheel puts the mover in the advantage seat, sets geopolitical standards, etc.
Refute side
As recorded by Table 6 (refute's own worksheet):
   * Let's take the proposition that whoever is first in AI will dominate everything else
   * That is an incredibly strong, incredibly general claim, which on the face of it is just completely implausible
   * "Dominate everything else." There is more to the world than economics and science. How likely are those two cases? There are a few examples of a single country or company dominating a single product. There is no precedent for one thing managing to dominate such varied industries.
   * There are lots of types of AI. If you can't buy an American-made motor, what is the chance of American dominance in robotics? There would be multiple winners and important players, not dominance.
   * Initially, DeepMind was the breakthrough. But this is no longer the dominant product. They are clearly behind Anthropic and OpenAI. So we've already disproved the extension of domination.
As recorded by Table 1 (affirm's worksheet, recording refute's claims):
   * Historically, dominance over "everything" has not been done
   * E.g., China good at robotics / US wins in software
As recorded by Table 4 ("First movers" — CON, "CM as the expert"):
   * Take the proposition that whoever is first will dominate everything else; encourage people to think this is an incredibly strong and general claim that is completely implausible
   * Break down the pieces
   * Not only economics and science — but even if you bought that definition, how likely is that to happen?
   * Few cases in history of a country or company dominating one thing — Bell in NA for decades, China dominates solar
   * No precedent for anyone dominating EVERYTHING
   * People think narrowly about AI, but there are things like robotics; if you can't build motors, what's the chance of winning in robotics?
   * How are we defining first in AI? Reasonable definition = DeepMind in neuro AI
   * Just not the case that Google DeepMind is dominating in this moment — SO ALREADY DISPROVEN
As recorded by Table 5 (supplementary, Debate 1):
   * Chris: Whoever dominates AI will dominate everything else — no, because there is more than technology and science; no, because AI domination does not mean domination in hard-technology sectors; the initial AI winners are already beginning to waver
As recorded by Table 3 (notes on debates):
   * Con: few historical examples of any technology enabling domination of multiple things
Rebuttals
Affirm rebuttal (Table 6):
   * The leading group would be a nation-state, not a company
   * Emphasizes the compounding effects within and between domains
   * Flywheel effect of attracting talent and investment will compound
   * Being "first" gives soft power to geopolitics and leadership. Leadership in AI sets language, writes history, educates children — and that is in fact everything else
   * Durable, compounding is at the ecosystem level, not product or company
Affirm rebuttal (Table 4 — PRO rebuttal):
   * Fundamental argument that "everything else" is such a broad category
   * AI has compounding advantage within and across domains
   * Sparking ideas, setting standards, attracting capital
   * Also matters in geopolitics and soft power — WW2 US power came from economic and military leadership; that in AI sets language and educates children
   * Yes, DeepMind had a moment of leadership, but we defined it as a durable compounding advantage, which is at the ECOSYSTEM level, not just one company or person
Refute rebuttal (Table 6):
   * What the affirm has to rebut is what was put forward in the AI 2027 document, which outlines a rapid-takeoff scenario in which AI provides a compounding advantage. The thought was it compounded so quickly that next year would be the end of the world.
   * This is inherently an "end of history" position
   * Look what has happened everywhere else
Refute rebuttal (Table 4 — CON rebuttal):
   * What PRO has to argue is the AI 2027 document — outlines the rapid-takeoff scenario, a compound advantage of AI
   * They thought it compounded so quickly that next year will be the end of the world (written last year) — pretty extreme beliefs
   * Have to be arguing an "end of history" position
   * SF AI APOCALYPTIC CULT
   * Not plausible — yes, for a while people will have a compound advantage, but it doesn't last: American manufacturing, Italian glasswork, etc.

   * Collaboration: one voice among many; relative decline, but managed

   * Keep decarbonization incentives even if geoengineering exists, so "drill baby drill" doesn't win by default
